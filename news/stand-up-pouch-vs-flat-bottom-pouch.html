    Stand-Up Pouch vs. Flat-Bottom Pouch: Which Fits Your Brand?

    Stand-up pouches and flat-bottom box pouches both stand on the shelf, both reseal with a zipper and both can be printed edge to edge with your brand. But they look, fill and sell differently. Here is how to choose between them.

    Two pouches that stand

    A stand-up pouch has a front panel, a back panel and a gusset folded into the bottom. When the pouch is filled, the gusset opens into an oval base and the pouch stands upright, with the front panel facing the shopper. The top stays flat, and the shape tapers from a wide base to a narrower top.

    A flat-bottom box pouch has a flat, rectangular base and gussets on both sides. Filled, it stands like a box, with straight sides and square corners. It has five printable panels: front, back, two sides and the bottom.

    How they look on the shelf

    Stand-up pouches have a softer, familiar shape. They are the everyday pouch for snacks, candy, dried fruit, tea, spices and pet treats, and shoppers know exactly how to use them. They look good in a row on a shelf or in a display box.

    Flat-bottom box pouches look more like a carton. The straight sides and flat front read as premium, and the pouch holds its shape well on the shelf. That is why you see them on specialty coffee, granola and larger bags of pet food, where the package has to stand out among other premium products.

    Neither is better in every case. The question is which shape matches your product and the way you want your brand to come across.

    Print area and branding

    On a stand-up pouch, the front panel does the selling and the back panel carries ingredients, nutrition facts and the barcode. The bottom gusset can be printed too, but it is mostly hidden on the shelf.

    A flat-bottom box pouch gives you more visible space. The side gussets face out when the pouch is turned or stacked, so they are useful for your story, brewing or feeding instructions, or a second look at the logo. The front stays clean, and the details move to the sides.

    Both are printed in up to 8 colors on our flexo presses, which is enough for a full brand palette, solid backgrounds and small, sharp type.

    Fill size and product fit

    Stand-up pouches work well across a wide range of fill sizes and are especially common for smaller and mid-size fills. Because they taper toward the top, they suit products that settle into the base, like granules, pieces and loose snacks.

    Flat-bottom box pouches make the most of their volume because the box shape carries the product all the way up. For larger fills, they can hold the same amount in a smaller footprint and stand more steadily, which helps on crowded shelves.

    If you are not sure, the easiest test is to fill a sample of each with your real product and put them side by side.

    Closures and features

    Both styles take a press-to-close zipper for products that are opened and closed more than once, and both can be made with features that suit the product: Anti-Fog for chilled foods, UV protection for products under bright store lights, Cold Seal for heat-sensitive products, and Anti-Static for fine powders or components. For food, both are made with FDA-compliant materials.

    Filling and packing

    Stand-up pouches are simple to fill by hand or on machines, which makes them easy to start with. Flat-bottom box pouches have a more structured shape, so it is worth checking how they will open and fill on your line before you commit. Tell your supplier how you fill today, and they can help you plan.

    A quick way to decide

    Choose a stand-up pouch if:

    • Your product is a familiar everyday item, like snacks, candy, tea or treats.
    • You want a proven, flexible format that is simple to fill.
    • Most of your selling happens on the front panel.

    Choose a flat-bottom box pouch if:

    • You want a premium, carton-like look on the shelf.
    • Your fills are larger and you want the pouch to stand steady and square.
    • You want extra printed panels on the sides for your story or instructions.

    For heavier products that need a very structured shape, also look at quad-seal pouches, which have a seal on each vertical corner.
